Overview

From Jobsite Navigation Hazards to Reproducible Simulation

Construction robots are increasingly used for inspection, progress monitoring, and site logistics, but current navigation stacks are often evaluated in clean or static settings. Real jobsites are different: terrain changes, materials move, workers and machines create temporary exclusion zones, and a robot can fail even when it avoids direct collision.

This benchmark turns construction-specific navigation hazards into controlled, repeatable simulation tasks. The goal is to evaluate whether a robot can reach its destination while respecting site safety rules, equipment work envelopes, access routes, material staging areas, and ongoing construction activities.

01

Construction-aware safety

Safety is defined beyond collision avoidance: robots should avoid interfering with work, entering restricted zones, blocking access paths, or moving through equipment operating areas.

02

Perception-centered hazards

The benchmark targets failures in self-perception, semantic understanding, affordance reasoning, and context awareness observed during real construction-site robot navigation.

03

Multi-platform simulation

The environment supports robots and construction equipment, including quadrupeds, forklifts, wheel loaders, excavators, trucks, and tower cranes.

04

Benchmark-ready structure

Scenes, tasks, safety rules, synthetic data collection, and held-out testing splits are organized for repeatable comparison of construction navigation methods.

Motivation

Why Construction Navigation Needs Its Own Benchmark

Geometry is not enough

On active jobsites, a traversable-looking surface may be unsafe, unfinished, reserved for work, or functionally blocked by temporary material staging.

Hazards are contextual

A route can become invalid because of a moving truck, active lifting operation, worker activity, temporary barrier, or changing construction sequence.

Success is not just arrival

A robot should be evaluated on whether it reaches the target while respecting safety rules, protecting site elements, and minimizing disruption to construction operations.

Environment library

Scenes, Assets, Robots, and Construction Equipment

The virtual environment is built around the operational features that make construction navigation difficult: unfinished structures, staged materials, temporary routes, active equipment, uneven terrain, and indoor-outdoor transitions.

Benchmark protocol

Navigation Task Suites and Evaluation Protocol

The benchmark focuses on construction-aware navigation. Agents are evaluated in simulated jobsites with changing layouts, temporary safety constraints, staged materials, and nearby construction equipment.

Map-Based Navigation

  • Navigate from start to goal using a site map or waypoint graph
  • Respect walkable zones, site boundaries, and temporary closures
  • Measure progress in known and partially updated maps

Instruction Navigation

  • Follow natural-language instructions grounded in construction semantics
  • Resolve targets such as workface, access gate, material laydown area, or equipment zone
  • Handle route constraints expressed as safety or work-sequence instructions

Object and Asset Navigation

  • Navigate to construction objects by category or instance
  • Find equipment, pallets, pipes, scaffolds, doors, trucks, and staging areas
  • Test object permanence and semantic recognition under clutter

Safety-Rule Navigation

  • Avoid crane swing radius, equipment blind spots, excavation edges, and exclusion zones
  • Yield to active work and avoid blocking workers, trucks, or material flow
  • Penalize rule violations even when the robot reaches the goal

Metrics

Success rate, path efficiency, SPL, completion time, rule violations, proximity events, stuck events, work-interference events, and context-sensitive safety failures.

Splits

Synthetic data collection, model development, validation, model testing, held-out layouts, and stress tests with unseen material placement and equipment activity.

Embodiments

Quadruped robots and site equipment such as forklifts, wheel loaders, excavators, trucks, and tower cranes can appear as agents, obstacles, or operational context.
